# Africa Fiscal Desk — Research notes

**Collection started:** 6 October 2026  
**Last reviewed:** 19 October 2026  
**Scope:** Fiscal policies, fiscal performance, and tax / non-tax domestic revenue mobilisation in African countries.

This is a curated starting library, not an exhaustive bibliography and not a live monitoring service. The companion [landing page](index.html) provides searchable cards and short evidence notes. Additions should record publication year, data reference period, country coverage, methods, and limitations. Prefer original institutional publications, official datasets, and peer-reviewed research.

## Current synthesis

- **Fiscal policy is a financing and allocation question.** Revenue mobilisation, expenditure efficiency, debt management and credible public investment management jointly determine fiscal space. AfDB's 2026 African Economic Outlook calls for coordinated strategies to mobilise development finance at scale amid fragmentation, while the latest IMF October 2026 SSA update projects 4.5% regional growth in both 2026 and 2027 and warns that high interest burdens, low reserves and falling aid constrain buffers. This is a regional forecast, not a country-level outcome.
- **Potential is not performance.** AfDB's 2026 AEO says Africa could unlock up to USD 1.43 trillion annually through a broad package: improved revenue collection, more efficient public investment, curbing illicit financial flows and corruption, deeper capital markets, PPPs, diaspora financing and natural capital. This is an estimated potential, not observed or guaranteed annual tax revenue; it also broadens the framing used in the 2025 AEO.
- **Comparable revenue series have a lag.** The latest OECD/AUC/ATAF *Revenue Statistics in Africa 2025* covers 38 countries and reports through 2023. The unweighted average tax-to-GDP ratio reached 16.1% in 2023, up 0.5 percentage points; VAT averaged 4.5% of GDP and taxes on goods and services 8.1%. These are covered-country averages, not a census of Africa. Social contribution gaps, fiscal calendars, GDP revisions, and national classifications affect comparability. The 2024 report's 36-country/2022 values are superseded for headline currency.
- **Regional averages conceal country differences.** Peer-reviewed comparative work finds revenue trajectories, tax effort and volatility vary with income level and institutional context. Country and regional comparisons should use the underlying table and its precise definition.
- **Administration, trust and governance matter.** ATAF's *African Tax Outlook 2025* and research portfolio track administration, taxpayer service and compliance. IMF's 2025 tax-expenditure survey found that of 45 SSA country teams surveyed, 32 reported information or estimates, but only 17 had what IMF considered comprehensive inventories and costing; the reported average cost among reporting countries was about 3% of GDP and estimates are not directly comparable. IMF's October 2026 Fiscal Monitor argues better designed VAT/corporate taxes, stronger work incentives, targeted innovation support and stronger administration can support growth without sacrificing revenue. Digital tools or tax-rate changes alone do not establish durable revenue gains.
- **Track a wider dashboard.** Recommended indicators include tax and non-tax revenue (GDP share and real growth), resource versus non-resource revenue, tax composition, revenue forecasts and out-turns, tax expenditures, compliance gaps, arrears/refunds, primary and overall balances, interest-to-revenue, spending composition and execution, investment efficiency, and subnational revenue. Interpret every indicator against country institutions and shocks.
- **External debt is an important fiscal-intelligence input.** The latest World Bank *International Debt Report 2025* analyzes end-2024 external debt stocks and flows; the IDS database is updated separately (catalog metadata refreshed 14 May 2026). These annual data complement revenue and deficit datasets by showing financing exposure and repayment pressure. The reporting population is low- and middle-income countries reporting external debt through the Debtor Reporting System; it is not a comprehensive measure of total public debt, domestic debt, fiscal balance, or near-real-time financing. Pair it with IMF fiscal series and country debt bulletins.
- **Investment and procurement leads need a separate evidence stream.** AfDB procurement notices and MapAfrica and World Bank project/document/procurement sources can surface disclosed tenders and project-stage opportunities. These are not a complete private investment pipeline and are not investment recommendations. Show primary-source link, issuer, country, status, eligibility, deadline and last checked date; verify every notice at issuer before acting.

## Annotated bibliography

### Multilateral and African institutions

1. **African Development Bank (2026). _African Economic Outlook 2026: Mobilizing Africa's Development Financing at Scale in a Fragmented World._** [Publication page](https://www.afdb.org/en/knowledge/publications/african-economic-outlook) · [full report](https://www.afdb.org/sites/default/files/documents/publications/afdb26-04_aeo_complete_english_0525.pdf). Latest continental financing outlook. It states Africa could unlock up to USD 1.43 trillion annually through a broad set of reforms; treat as potential, not realized receipts, and consult definitions before reusing the figure.
2. **IMF (2026). _Fiscal Monitor, October 2026: Taxing Better to Boost Growth._** [IMF publication](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/fm/issues/2026/10/05/fiscal-monitor-october-2026) · [eLibrary chapter](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9798229056298/9798229056298.xml). Special edition on tax design, growth and revenue; its findings concern design channels and should be applied with country-level distributional and administrative analysis.
3. **IMF (2026). _Regional Economic Outlook Update, Sub-Saharan Africa, October 2026: Resilient Growth, Clouded Outlook._** [IMF report](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/reo/ssa/issues/2026/10/15/regional-economic-outlook-for-sub-saharan-africa-october-2026) · [IMF data series](https://data.imf.org/en/datasets/AFRREO). Projects 4.5% growth in 2026 and 2027; emphasizes country heterogeneity, debt-service burdens, reserves and aid constraints. Forecasts are not actual outcomes.
4. **AfDB (2025). _African Economic Outlook 2025._** [Report page](https://www.afdb.org/en/documents/african-economic-outlook-2025). Earlier framing of the USD 1.43 trillion potential; the 2026 AEO provides a broader set of channels. Treat as potential, not an observed revenue outcome.
5. **OECD/AUC/ATAF (2025). _Revenue Statistics in Africa 2025: Commonalities and Specificities across African Revenue Classifications._** [Report](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/revenue-statistics-in-africa-2025_8d3bf3af-en.html) · [full report](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/revenue-statistics-in-africa-2025_8d3bf3af-en/full-report.html) · [data explorer](https://data-explorer.oecd.org/s/dx). Comparable tax/non-tax statistics for 38 countries through 2023. The unweighted average tax-to-GDP ratio was 16.1% in 2023, up 0.5 percentage points; VAT was 4.5% and total taxes on goods and services 8.1% of GDP on average. Social contribution gaps, fiscal calendars, GDP revisions, and reporting differences warrant caution.
6. **ATAF. _African Tax Outlook 2025, Research and ATAF Databank._** [ATAF library](https://ataftax.org/library/) · [research portal](https://ataftax.org/research/). African-led tax administration indicators and applied research. Check each series' participating-country coverage and reference period; do not assume that annual publication means every indicator is current-year data.
7. **Global Forum / ATAF (2026). _Tax Transparency in Africa 2026._** [ATAF announcement](https://ataftax.org/news/new-report-highlights-africas-growing-progress-in-tax-transparency/). Tracks tax transparency and exchange-of-information progress. Transparency is an enforcement enabler; do not equate implementation outputs with revenue collected without additional evidence.
8. **IMF (2025). _Tax Expenditures in Sub-Saharan Africa._ Departmental Paper 2025/006.** [PDF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/dp/2025/english/tessaea.pdf). Reviews tax expenditures, incentives and mobilisation. Reforms should be evaluated for fiscal cost, distribution, investment effects and administrative feasibility in each country.
9. **World Bank. _International Debt Report 2025 / International Debt Statistics._** [IDR 2025 and data](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/debt-statistics/idr/products) · [IDS portal](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/debt-statistics/ids) · [DataBank](https://databank.worldbank.org/source/international-debt-statistics) · [API guide](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/889392-about-the-indicators-api-documentation). IDR 2025 analyzes end-2024 external debt stocks and flows. Annual external-debt data for DRS-reporting low- and middle-income countries; not a whole-of-government debt or fiscal dataset. Database refreshes can differ from report publication dates.
10. **OECD Data Explorer SDMX API.** [Africa revenue dataset](https://data-explorer.oecd.org/s/dx) · [API guide](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2024/09/api.html). Programmatic SDMX access (JSON, XML or CSV) to comparative tax and non-tax revenue statistics.
11. **IMF Data APIs and DataMapper.** [IMF API overview](https://data.imf.org/en/Resource-Pages/IMF-API) · [DataMapper API documentation](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/api/) · [Fiscal Monitor dataset](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/datasets/FM) · [WEO dataset](https://data.imf.org/en/datasets/IMF.RES%3AWEO). IMF Data supports SDMX 2.1/3.0; DataMapper exposes selected JSON series. Label dataset vintage, reference period, estimate/forecast status and country coverage; IMF estimates can differ from national or other IMF datasets.
12. **World Bank Projects & Documents API.** [API documentation](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/api) · [World Bank project procurement framework](https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/what-we-do/project-procurement/framework). Can help surface disclosed projects, documents and procurement plans. Validate live notices, status, country eligibility and deadlines against source material.
13. **African Development Bank procurement sources.** [Procurement notices](https://www.afdb.org/en/projects-and-operations/procurement) · [resources for businesses](https://www.afdb.org/en/projects-and-operations/procurement/resources-for-businesses) · [MapAfrica](https://mapafrica.afdb.org/en/). Useful for official tenders, expressions of interest, awards and project context; no verified public API was identified in this review, so link or ingest only through an approved feed/terms-compliant method.

### Peer-reviewed scholarship

14. **Gwaindepi, A. (2021). “Domestic revenue mobilisation in developing countries: An exploratory analysis of sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America.” _Journal of International Development_, 33, 396–421.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jid.3528). Comparative historical and panel analysis; highlights cross-country heterogeneity, higher revenue volatility in SSA in the sample, and the importance of institutional and income-level context. Study period begins in 1980; it is not a current estimate.
15. **Ofori, P. E., Ofori, I. K. & Asongu, S. A. (2022). “Towards efforts to enhance tax revenue mobilisation in Africa: Exploring the interaction between industrialisation and digital infrastructure.” _Telematics and Informatics_, 72, 101857.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tele.2022.101857). Study of 42 African countries, 1996–2020, on industrialisation/digital infrastructure interactions. Its estimates are conditional and should not be read as proof that digitalisation alone raises revenue.
16. **“Quality of governance and tax revenue mobilization in Africa.” (2026). _African Journal of Economic and Management Studies_, 17(3), 405–421.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1108/AJEMS-10-2023-0391). Sys-GMM analysis of 30 countries over 2002–2021; examines governance and interactions with agriculture and natural-resource rents. Check full methods and robustness before drawing causal conclusions.
17. **Mabugu, R. E. & Rakabe, M. (2026). “Domestic Revenue Mobilisation in Zambia: A Case Study of Tax Administration and Fiscal Governance.” _Administrative Sciences_, 16(9), 447.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/admsci16090447). Qualitative case study with descriptive 2020–2024 data; reports tax revenue rising from 15.4% of GDP in 2020 to 18.8% in 2024, while cautioning that the change cannot be attributed automatically to administrative efficiency. Its country-specific claims should not be generalized to the continent.

## Indicator and citation discipline

- Always label **tax revenue** separately from **total revenue**; grants, social contributions and natural-resource receipts may be classified differently.
- A tax-to-GDP ratio is not a tax rate, a measure of fairness, or by itself a measure of effort. Ratios can change when GDP is rebased or revised.
- Preserve the source's **country set, reporting year, fiscal-year convention, average weighting and denominator**.
- Separate forecasts, estimated potential, announced measures, tax assessments and cash collected.
- Do not infer causality from descriptive changes or associations in a panel regression.
- Country fiscal performance requires official budget execution and revenue-authority data alongside cross-country databases. Record the release date and revisions whenever adding them.
