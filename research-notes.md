# Africa Fiscal Desk — Research notes

**Collection started:** 6 October 2026  
**Scope:** Fiscal policies, fiscal performance, and tax / non-tax domestic revenue mobilisation in African countries.

This is a curated starting library, not an exhaustive bibliography and not a live monitoring service. The companion [landing page](index.html) provides searchable cards and short evidence notes. Additions should record publication year, data reference period, country coverage, methods, and limitations. Prefer original institutional publications, official datasets, and peer-reviewed research.

## Current synthesis

- **Fiscal policy is a financing and allocation question.** Revenue mobilisation, expenditure efficiency, debt management and credible public investment management jointly determine fiscal space. AfDB's 2026 African Economic Outlook focuses on mobilising development finance at scale in a fragmented global context.
- **Potential is not performance.** AfDB's 2025 AEO estimated an additional USD 1.43 trillion could be mobilised from domestic tax and non-tax resources through efficiency gains and curbing leakages. This should be described as estimated potential, not collected revenue.
- **Comparable revenue series have a lag.** OECD/AUC/ATAF's 2024 Revenue Statistics in Africa covers 36 countries and reports data through 2022. It reports average VAT revenue of 4.5% of GDP in 2022 for the covered-country Africa average. Coverage for social contributions is incomplete in some countries; country fiscal calendars, GDP revisions and reporting conventions affect comparability.
- **Regional averages conceal country differences.** Peer-reviewed comparative work finds revenue trajectories, tax effort and volatility vary with income level and institutional context. Country and regional comparisons should use the underlying table and its precise definition.
- **Administration, trust and governance matter.** ATAF's research portfolio tracks administration, taxpayer service and compliance. IMF work treats informality, enforcement capacity, exemptions and implementation as important constraints. Digital tools or tax-rate changes alone do not establish durable revenue gains.
- **Track a wider dashboard.** Recommended indicators include tax and non-tax revenue (GDP share and real growth), resource versus non-resource revenue, tax composition, revenue forecasts and out-turns, tax expenditures, compliance gaps, arrears/refunds, primary and overall balances, interest-to-revenue, spending composition and execution, investment efficiency, and subnational revenue. Interpret every indicator against country institutions and shocks.
- **External debt is an important fiscal-intelligence input.** World Bank IDS is a strong addition for annual external debt stocks/flows, debt-service measures, creditor composition and external-debt ratios. It complements revenue and deficit datasets by showing financing exposure and repayment pressure. Its population is low- and middle-income countries reporting public and publicly guaranteed external debt through the Debtor Reporting System; it is not a comprehensive measure of total public debt, domestic debt, fiscal balance, or near-real-time financing. Pair it with IMF fiscal series and country debt bulletins.
- **Investment and procurement leads need a separate evidence stream.** AfDB procurement notices and MapAfrica and World Bank project/document/procurement sources can surface disclosed tenders and project-stage opportunities. These are not a complete private investment pipeline and are not investment recommendations. Show primary-source link, issuer, country, status, eligibility, deadline and last checked date; verify every notice at issuer before acting.

## Annotated bibliography

### Multilateral and African institutions

1. **African Development Bank (2026). _African Economic Outlook 2026: Mobilizing Africa's Development Financing at Scale in a Fragmented World._** [Publication page](https://www.afdb.org/en/knowledge/publications/african-economic-outlook). Current continental financing outlook. Consult the report and highlights for the precise definitions, time series and fiscal chapters before citing individual figures.
2. **IMF (2026). _Fiscal Monitor: Fiscal Policy under Pressure: High Debt, Rising Risks._** [Report PDF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/fiscal-monitor/2026/april/english/text.pdf). Global fiscal risks and financing context; pair with regional and country analyses for Africa-specific conclusions.
3. **IMF (2025). _Regional Economic Outlook: Sub-Saharan Africa, October 2025 — Holding Steady._** [eLibrary report](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/display/book/9798229026178/9798229026178.xml). Regional framing identifies domestic revenue mobilisation and debt management as priorities amid constrained finance; discusses practical implementation constraints.
4. **AfDB (2025). _African Economic Outlook 2025._** [Report page](https://www.afdb.org/en/documents/african-economic-outlook-2025). Estimates USD 1.43 trillion of additional domestic resource potential from efficiency gains, tax/non-tax mobilisation and reduced leakages. Treat as a potential estimate, not an observed revenue outcome.
5. **OECD/AUC/ATAF (2024). _Revenue Statistics in Africa 2024: Facilitation and Trust as Drivers of Voluntary Tax Compliance._** [Report](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/revenue-statistics-in-africa-2024_78e9af3a-en.html) · [full report](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/revenue-statistics-in-africa-2024_78e9af3a-en/full-report.html) · [data explorer](https://data-explorer.oecd.org/s/dx). Comparable tax/non-tax statistics for 36 countries; reported data through 2022. Country definitions, revision histories and fiscal-year notes are essential.
6. **ATAF. _Research, African Tax Outlook and ATAF Databank._** [Research portal](https://ataftax.org/research/). African-led tax administration indicators and applied research. The described African Tax Outlook compares participating countries across tax rates, bases, structure, revenue performance, administration, taxpayer service and compliance.
7. **Global Forum / ATAF (2026). _Tax Transparency in Africa 2026._** [ATAF announcement](https://ataftax.org/news/new-report-highlights-africas-growing-progress-in-tax-transparency/). Tracks tax transparency and exchange-of-information progress. Transparency is an enforcement enabler; do not equate implementation outputs with revenue collected without additional evidence.
8. **IMF (2025). _Tax Expenditures in Sub-Saharan Africa._ Departmental Paper 2025/006.** [PDF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/dp/2025/english/tessaea.pdf). Reviews tax expenditures, incentives and mobilisation. Reforms should be evaluated for fiscal cost, distribution, investment effects and administrative feasibility in each country.
9. **World Bank. _International Debt Statistics (IDS)._** [IDS portal](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/debt-statistics/ids) · [IDS DataBank](https://databank.worldbank.org/source/international-debt-statistics) · [World Bank Indicators API v2 guide](https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/889392-about-the-indicators-api-documentation). Annual external debt stocks/flows, ratios and creditor composition for DRS-reporting low- and middle-income countries. Strong complement to fiscal data for debt-service and external vulnerability monitoring; not a whole-of-government debt or fiscal dataset. World Bank user guide also links [API examples](https://github.com/worldbank/IDS-API-Examples) and [methodology](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/debt-statistics/methodology).
10. **OECD Data Explorer SDMX API.** [Africa revenue dataset](https://data-explorer.oecd.org/s/dx) · [API guide](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/insights/data-explainers/2024/09/api.html). Programmatic SDMX access (JSON, XML or CSV) to comparative tax and non-tax revenue statistics.
11. **IMF Data APIs and DataMapper.** [IMF API overview](https://data.imf.org/en/Resource-Pages/IMF-API) · [DataMapper API documentation](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/api/) · [Fiscal Monitor dataset](https://www.imf.org/external/datamapper/datasets/FM) · [WEO dataset](https://data.imf.org/en/datasets/IMF.RES%3AWEO). IMF Data supports SDMX 2.1/3.0; DataMapper exposes selected JSON series. Current API explorer may require a portal account. Fiscal Monitor metadata warns its estimates can differ from official data and other IMF datasets.
12. **World Bank Projects & Documents API.** [API documentation](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/api) · [World Bank project procurement framework](https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/what-we-do/project-procurement/framework). Can help surface disclosed projects, documents and procurement plans. Validate live notices, status, country eligibility and deadlines against source material.
13. **African Development Bank procurement sources.** [Procurement notices](https://www.afdb.org/en/projects-and-operations/procurement) · [resources for businesses](https://www.afdb.org/en/projects-and-operations/procurement/resources-for-businesses) · [MapAfrica](https://mapafrica.afdb.org/en/). Useful for official tenders, expressions of interest, awards and project context; no verified public API was identified in this review, so link or ingest only through an approved feed/terms-compliant method.

### Peer-reviewed scholarship

14. **Gwaindepi, A. (2021). “Domestic revenue mobilisation in developing countries: An exploratory analysis of sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America.” _Journal of International Development_, 33, 396–421.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jid.3528). Comparative historical and panel analysis; highlights cross-country heterogeneity, higher revenue volatility in SSA in the sample, and the importance of institutional and income-level context. Study period begins in 1980; it is not a current estimate.
15. **Ofori, P. E., Ofori, I. K. & Asongu, S. A. (2022). “Towards efforts to enhance tax revenue mobilisation in Africa: Exploring the interaction between industrialisation and digital infrastructure.” _Telematics and Informatics_, 72, 101857.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tele.2022.101857). Study of 42 African countries, 1996–2020, on industrialisation/digital infrastructure interactions. Its estimates are conditional and should not be read as proof that digitalisation alone raises revenue.
16. **“Quality of governance and tax revenue mobilization in Africa.” (2026). _African Journal of Economic and Management Studies_, 17(3), 405–421.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1108/AJEMS-10-2023-0391). Sys-GMM analysis of 30 countries over 2002–2021; examines governance and interactions with agriculture and natural-resource rents. Check full methods and robustness before drawing causal conclusions.
17. **Phiri, M. (2026). “Domestic Revenue Mobilisation in Zambia: A Case Study of Tax Administration and Fiscal Governance.” _Administrative Sciences_, 16(9), 447.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/admsci16090447). Qualitative case study with descriptive 2020–2024 data; discusses mining taxation, VAT administration, e-invoicing, trust and expenditure credibility. Its country-specific claims should not be generalized to the continent.

## Indicator and citation discipline

- Always label **tax revenue** separately from **total revenue**; grants, social contributions and natural-resource receipts may be classified differently.
- A tax-to-GDP ratio is not a tax rate, a measure of fairness, or by itself a measure of effort. Ratios can change when GDP is rebased or revised.
- Preserve the source's **country set, reporting year, fiscal-year convention, average weighting and denominator**.
- Separate forecasts, estimated potential, announced measures, tax assessments and cash collected.
- Do not infer causality from descriptive changes or associations in a panel regression.
- Country fiscal performance requires official budget execution and revenue-authority data alongside cross-country databases. Record the release date and revisions whenever adding them.
